ai-visibility-audit

What an AI assistant can actually read on your site

An assistant's crawler fetches HTML and stops there: it renders no JavaScript and infers nothing. A site built for Google can therefore be invisible to it while every SEO tool reports the site as fine - a robots.txt copied from a template years ago blocks GPTBot by name, or the page ships forty words of markup behind nine hundred kilobytes of script. This prints the specific things standing between a page and being quoted.

Source on GitHub Work with me

What it does

  • Reads robots.txt as the assistants' crawlers read it, per named agent, so a blanket rule and a rule aimed at one bot are told apart.
  • Parses llms.txt and follows its links: a guide that points at pages that are gone is worse than no guide, and that is the common case.
  • Judges each page on what is in the HTML - word count, who published it, usable structured data, whether the copy answers a question anyone asks.
  • Every check runs offline in the self-test, so a change to the scoring cannot pass unnoticed.

How it is proved

31 of 31 offline self-checks pass, each pinning one rule, and the same audit found the survey finding published on this site: of the top 1,500 sites, one in ten that publish an llms.txt also ban a crawler that would read it.
