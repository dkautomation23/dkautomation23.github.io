linkscan

The broken links, without the false alarms

Run an ordinary link checker against an ordinary site and the report fills with 403s from Cloudflare, 999s from LinkedIn and timeouts from a slow CDN, none of which are broken links. After the second false alarm nobody opens the report again, which is the same as not having one. This separates a link a visitor would find dead from a link a bot was refused.

Source on GitHub Work with me

What it does

  • Three outcomes, not one: broken (404, 410, a 5xx a visitor would see), could not verify (refused or timed out), and works but sends your traffic off your domain. Only the first sets a non-zero exit code.
  • Every unique URL is checked once however many pages point at it, and every one of those pages is remembered, so the report says where to go and fix it.
  • HEAD first, GET when HEAD is refused - plenty of servers answer 405 to HEAD while serving GET perfectly.
  • A 404 on a domain's root is never a deleted page: crates.io answers 404 to anything that is not a browser, and the first version duly reported the Rust homepage as linking to a dead site. Both rules that fixed it are covered by tests.

How it is proved

Six pages of rust-lang.org, 220 unique links checked in 10.5 seconds at 24 concurrent requests. 28 tests, none of which touch the internet.
